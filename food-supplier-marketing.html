Useful details help people decide.

Clarify the product and audience.

Explain what the business offers, who it serves and how enquiries are handled. Confirm product descriptions, packaging information, availability and order requirements with the business. Content marketing can help turn those answers into useful public information.

Make the presentation recognisable.

Product photographs, menus, packaging and social posts should feel like the same business. Our brand identity and graphic design services can support that presentation where agreed. Only use photographs and customer material the business has permission to publish.

Keep the next step practical.

A clear food-business website or public information page can explain how to request current availability or a quote. For local businesses, accurate local search information may also matter. Do not publish delivery areas, lead times, certifications or export claims until the responsible business has confirmed them.

Content should fit the way you sell.

A catering business may plan around events and bookings; grocery or packaged-food retail may need regular product updates; a sourcing/export business may need more explanation before an order conversation. Social media planning and management should reflect those differences, available source material and approval responsibilities.

We discuss audiences and campaign budgets after the offer and destination are clear. Food production, allergen/product information, fulfilment, storage, shipping and regulatory responsibilities stay with the relevant business and its advisers. Marketing supports an accurate customer journey; it does not replace those operational decisions.

Before we start.

Do you arrange sourcing, shipping or export approval?

This page describes digital marketing support. The food business and its providers handle sourcing, fulfilment, shipping and regulatory requirements.

Can the approach work for catering as well as packaged food?

The capabilities can be relevant, but the brief differs. We first understand whether customers need an event quote, a delivery order, wholesale information or a sourcing conversation.

Will social content guarantee orders?

No. Demand, product suitability, pricing, availability, market conditions, response and fulfilment all affect outcomes. We agree marketing work without guaranteed orders.

What information should we bring?

Bring public product information, target audiences, common questions and approved photographs. Your team should confirm descriptions, availability, delivery and any claims before publication.
