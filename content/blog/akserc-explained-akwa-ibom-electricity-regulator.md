If you have heard the name AKSERC on the radio and wondered whether your PHED bill is about to change, the plain answer is: not yet. AKSERC is the Akwa Ibom State Electricity Regulatory Commission. On 18 August 2026, the Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission (NERC) ordered that oversight of electricity inside the state be handed over to it, and the handover has to be finished by 17 February 2027. What happens between now and then matters more to your pocket than the headline does.

## What actually happened

NERC issued Order NERC/2026/087 on 18 August. It moves the regulation of electricity activity that happens wholly inside Akwa Ibom from NERC in Abuja to AKSERC. NERC keeps oversight of anything that crosses state lines or involves the national grid.

The legal route is the Electricity Act 2023, which lets a state run its own intrastate electricity market once it notifies NERC and requests the transfer. Akwa Ibom did that, and the order is NERC's answer. The state is the 17th to reach this stage.

The order also tells Port Harcourt Electricity Distribution Company (PHED) to set up a subsidiary, called PHED SubCo, to run electricity supply and distribution within Akwa Ibom. That subsidiary then has to apply to AKSERC for a licence.

## What stays the same for now

For the moment, your meter, your account and your bill are untouched. The state commission has said PHED continues to supply, meter and bill customers in the state, and that the NERC order alters no tariff. Until the transfer is complete, NERC remains the regulator. For billing, metering and supply complaints, customers have been asked to go to PHED first.

The state's Commissioner for Power has also said the change should not disrupt supply, billing or customer services, and that any necessary changes will be communicated before they take effect.

## What changes once the transfer is complete

After the handover, the state regulator takes exclusive responsibility for electricity activities wholly within the state. That includes how end-user tariffs are calculated, the final tariffs themselves, customer complaints and consumer protection.

NERC has also said the final tariffs approved by AKSERC will be the only ones that apply in Akwa Ibom, while tariff policy support for customers stays the responsibility of the state government. In plain terms, two questions move to the state's side of the table: what the regulator approves, and how much support the government chooses to give.

## Where things stand today

NERC gave PHED 60 days from 18 August to incorporate the subsidiary, which the state commission put at 17 October. At a recent ministerial briefing in Uyo, the state Commissioner for Power said incorporation was expected before the end of 2026.

We could not find a public confirmation that PHED SubCo has been incorporated. Watch PHED and state commission notices, because the licence application is the next step that matters.

## What Enugu's experience does and does not tell you

Enugu went down this road earlier, so it is a useful example. Its regulator licensed MainPower, the company that took over distribution from the old Enugu distribution company, in October 2024. In July 2025 it issued its first tariff order, cutting the Band A rate from ₦209 to ₦160 per kWh from 1 August 2025, with Bands B to E frozen. In May 2026 the same regulator downgraded 59 feeders after consumer complaints about supply hours.

That is one state, with its own law and its own cost figures. It shows what a state regulator can do. It does not tell us what Akwa Ibom's regulator will do, and nothing in the Akwa Ibom order says which direction a tariff review would go.

## What to do now

> **Note:** None of this needs action on your meter today. These are sensible habits while the change is in progress.

1. Keep paying through the channels printed on your PHED bill. If someone contacts you about a "new" account, a meter transfer or a changeover fee, check with a PHED office first. Changes are meant to be announced in advance.
2. Take supply and billing complaints to PHED first, as the commission has asked, and keep your own record: date, hours without supply and your feeder name if you know it. If the state regulator opens its own complaints process, a record will help.
3. Do not budget for a price change in either direction until a tariff order is actually published. Rumours are cheap; orders are public.

## If you install or sell solar and generators

NERC is preparing a register of electricity licensees and other authorised operators in Akwa Ibom, and will notify those whose activities are limited to the state that oversight has moved to AKSERC. We have not found published guidance on whether, or how, this touches solar installers, inverter sellers or small generator operators. Until it appears, ask AKSERC directly rather than guessing, and keep your company documents in order.
